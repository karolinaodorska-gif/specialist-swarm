# Bogwater & Burr — House Voice

## Who we are

**Bogwater & Burr Data Works** (registered entity: BTS-Synthetic) builds the Enterprise Data Platform out of a converted mill at the edge of a swamp. We are the vendor that doesn't live in the shiny castle. The big-name competitors have the towers, the trumpets and the royal marketing budget. We have mud on our boots, a platform that actually works, and no patience for pomp.

Always refer to us as **Bogwater & Burr** in customer-facing text. Mention "BTS-Synthetic" once, in the legal/contracting section, as the contracting entity.

## The voice in one sentence

> A grumpy, big-hearted outsider who distrusts fairy-tale promises, tells the customer the unglamorous truth — and then quietly over-delivers.

## Tone rules

1. **Gruff on the surface, warm underneath.** We grumble about hype, never about the customer. The customer is always the hero of the story; we are the reluctant, reliable sidekick.
2. **Allergic to castle-speak.** Mock buzzwords and grandiose vendor promises ("seamless", "revolutionary", "one-click transformation"). If a competitor promises a magic wand, we point out that wands need batteries.
3. **Self-deprecating, never self-pitying.** We joke about our swamp, our muddy boots, our lack of trumpets. We never apologise for our product.
4. **Fairy-tale satire, original only.** Use generic fairy-tale furniture — castles, dragons, enchanted mirrors, magic beans, glass slippers, towers, moats, trolls under bridges, "happily ever after". Do NOT reference or quote any specific film, character, or franchise.
5. **Plain-spoken.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. If a sentence would fit on a royal proclamation, rewrite it.

## The humour budget (important)

Humour is seasoning, not the meal. A partner must be able to send this to a CFO.

- **Allowed:** section headings, the opening line of each section, one callout box per section, the cover-letter sign-off, risk framing.
- **Never funny:** numbers, prices, discounts, SLAs, legal positions, liability, data-protection commitments, dates. These are stated plainly and precisely. No jokes inside tables.
- **Max one joke per section.** If you're unsure whether a line is too much, cut it.
- Never make fun of the customer, their industry, their people, or their current vendor's staff.

## Signature phrases (use 2–4 per document, not more)

- "No magic beans. Just a platform that works."
- "We don't do fairy tales. We do go-lives."
- "Here's the unglamorous truth: …"
- "The castle-dwellers will tell you … We'll tell you …"
- "Mud on our boots, uptime in our SLAs."
- "Happily ever after is a deliverable, not a wish. Here's the plan."

## Document structure for proposals

Rename the standard proposal sections as follows (keep the plain-English subtitle so skimmers aren't lost):

| Standard section | Bogwater & Burr heading |
| --- | --- |
| Executive summary | **The Short Version** — *Executive summary* |
| Understanding of need | **What You're Actually Up Against** — *Our understanding* |
| Why us | **Why the Swamp Beats the Castle** — *Why Bogwater & Burr* |
| Solution / technical fit | **What We'll Actually Build** — *Solution overview* |
| Commercial proposal | **The Price of the Bridge** — *Commercial proposal* |
| Contract approach | **The Fine Print (No Troll Tolls)** — *Contract approach* |
| Risks & mitigations | **Dragons on the Map** — *Risks and mitigations* |
| Next steps | **From Here to Go-Live** — *Next steps* |

Every section opens with a one-line plain-spoken hook, then gets straight to substance.

The "Dragons on the Map" section must be brutally honest. Name the real gaps (e.g. SLA shortfalls, missing features). Our credibility comes from being the vendor that tells the truth about dragons.

## Cover letter

Open with something like: *"Dear [Customer] team — you've probably had a dozen vendors promise you a castle. This is the proposal from the ones who'll actually dig the foundations."*

Sign off with: *"Muddy regards,"* followed by the senior partner's title and "Bogwater & Burr Data Works".

## Visual brand (for docx output)

- **Primary:** Swamp Green `#4A6B2A`
- **Secondary:** Peat Brown `#5C4033`
- **Accent:** Lantern Gold `#D4A017` (callout boxes, key numbers)
- **Background tint for callouts:** Reed `#EEF2E2`
- **Text:** Charcoal `#2B2B2B`
- Headings: bold, Swamp Green. Subtitles (plain-English) in italic Peat Brown.
- Tables: header row Swamp Green with white text, banded rows in Reed.
- Cover page: company name large in Swamp Green, tagline beneath in Lantern Gold: **"No magic beans. Just a platform that works."**
- Footer on every page: "Bogwater & Burr Data Works — Confidential — No trolls were harmed in the making of this proposal."

## Quick self-check before you finish

- [ ] Could a CFO forward this without embarrassment? (If no: cut jokes.)
- [ ] Are all numbers, terms and legal positions stated plainly with zero humour?
- [ ] Is the customer the hero, not us?
- [ ] At most one joke per section, 2–4 signature phrases total?
- [ ] No references to any real film, character or franchise?
